## The verification ledger, round 6 — the audit's witness reproduced byte-for-byte, extended twice, and turned into the v1 identity theory

**1. GPT's witness is real, and it reproduces to the byte.** The audit answered the v0.5/v0.6 open question — is `floatDigest` invariant under heap-id permutation? — in the negative, with digests `18b4e47b34d38339…` vs `6bca1878284b4dfd…` on states it verified behaviorally identical. Probe D rebuilt the witness against the live kernel: **both digests match the audit's exactly**, and the behavioral identity is re-verified here (enabled `DUP-VAR, DUP-VAR`; NF `((X Y) (X Y))`; 2 steps; 2 interactions). The mechanism is `foldHeap`: it folds **all** heap entries in **id order**, so the fold order — a pure representation artifact — enters the digest. Two extensions found while probing: a **dead** heap entry also perturbs the digest while the live state is untouched; and a **monotone** allocator perturbation is *invisible* to sorted folds — the id VALUES canonicalize away and only their ORDER survives — so the separation witness requires an order-reversing allocator. That last fact designed the adversary for me: `DescFloatRt`, descending ids, stride 13.

**2. The resolution is the audit's own proposal, adopted whole: split, don't weaken.** `execStateId` (= `floatDigest`) stays exactly as it is — total heap, id-order fold, dead entries included — and is now *declared* allocation-sensitive: that sensitivity is what makes it replay-grade within one implementation and one allocator discipline, and films and certificate receipts bind it (`law:state.exec-identity@1`). `semStateId` is new: the **live** heap folded in **first-reachable discovery order** from the root, then the existing canonical digest — which was already α- and label-equivariant by first-occurrence numbering, so the quotient inherits those for free (`law:state.semantic-quotient@1`). L-SEMID-1 attacks the split with the audit's witness (asserted verbatim), dead-entry injection, α-renaming, label bijection, the descending allocator, and **mid-run random heap-id bijections** on a 10-step-deep exp state — semantic identity invariant throughout, execution identity varying alongside, which is the split *working*, not failing. L-SEMID-2 keeps the quotient honest in the other direction: 0 collisions across all corpus pairs, and the locked digest-adequacy pair (`λa.λb.a` vs `λa.λb.b`) separates at the semantic level too — sampled, under the stated SHA-256 assumption, exactly as L-ADEQ-2 phrases it.

**3. The ic32 bridge is executable today, and it is asymmetric in precisely the right way.** L-REFINE-1 runs every vector on both allocators in lockstep under a rotating free-choice schedule: **per-step semantic chains equal 24/24**, NFs reference-equal, interaction counts equal (`law:refine.alloc-portability@1`). The **semantic film** — canonical loci (`t:<path>` / `d:<discovery-ix>` / `v:<ix>:<path>`), semantic pre/post ids, a committed terminal under `TRVM-SEMFILM-v1` — is built on the ascending allocator and **replays on the descending one 24/24** by locus *matching* against the live enumeration, so enabledness is inherent: an unmatched locus refuses. The execution films do not travel: replayFloat accepts A's films 24/24 and **refuses B's on 17/24** (`revision-mismatch` — B's digests carry B's fold order), with the remaining **7 bit-identical** — single-dup states where fold order cannot differ. That refusal is not a defect; it is the *measurement* of what execution identity means. `refinement_receipt.json` records the asymmetry per term under a recomputable commitment (`receipt_id = H("TRVM-REFINE-v1" | per_term | summary)`), and this — semantic film in, locus-matched replay out — is the protocol ic32 must satisfy. The stand-in is adversarial, not foreign: the "declared not met by design" line in `v1_criteria` says so out loud.

**4. The certified battery is now **144 receipts** (6 × 24), all completed, NF-matched, readback-pure — and the worst case is **still 91 steps**: persistent starvation of a redex class did not even worsen the bound on this corpus (`law:sched.adversarial.float@1`). The certificate's committed strategy carries all six names; the checker re-executes every adversarial receipt like any other; grid_check now also checks the committed scheduler set against the grid's own `strategy_schedulers` declaration. Fairness evidence travels in the same receipts as freedom evidence.

**5. ## Theory movement this round

**Identity is now a stated quotient, twice over.** The round's law-shaped moral: *one digest is never forced to mean two things.* Execution identity and semantic identity have declared scopes, declared sensitivities, and separate binding sites — and the semantic quotient (live-only, discovery-ordered, id/dead/α/label/allocation-invariant) immediately paid for itself with the 24/24 cross-allocator chain equality. The COHERENCE meta-law's v0.5 obligation is resolved and its evidence line says how.

**Portability is a property of the evidence object, not the claim.** The same derivation yields two films: the execution film that replays only on its own allocator discipline, and the semantic film that replays anywhere the relation is implemented. L-REFINE-1's 17-refused / 7-identical split is the first *measured* boundary of execution-identity portability on this corpus.

**v1.0.0 is declared with its scope stated, not implied.** The calculus layer's law set is closed over its own claims: conformance, dual identity, free + adversarial progress, planes, both film families, receipt certificates, the refinement bridge, artifact identity, and self-falsification — each criterion pointing at a battery. WORLD/EFFECT, the σ profile, CP5–CP7 PhaseSpan, and Warrant v3 (with the round-5 shared support/footprint requirement) are the named layers above; the one criterion declared not met by design is the second implementation itself — the interface for which is what this round built.

**Next:** σ profile and CP5–CP7 on the TRVM side; Warrant v3 + WorldRecord with the shared footprint; and the ic32 implementation against the semantic-film protocol — the audit's "disproportionate unlock," now with a concrete replay target waiting for it.

## Round 6.1 — the closure audit's catch, closed as a law

**8. GPT falsified the semantic film's terminal contract, and the witness reproduced exactly.** `AUDIT-SEM-BUDGET`: a zero-frame `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` semantic film over `(λx.x X)` — one enabled redex — replayed `ok: true` under `TRVM-SEMFILM-v1`, and mutating `budget: 999 → 1`, `remaining_work: 424242 → −7` **did not change `film_id`** (`budget_mutation_preserves_id: true`, `r1.ok: true`, `r2.ok: true`, all reproduced against v1.0.0 before the fix). Two bugs composing: the commitment omitted both witness fields, and replay had no budget re-derivation branch at all — `if NORMAL_FORM: prove it; else: accept it`. The same species as the round-4 execution-film terminal hole, reintroduced by the **third** replay implementation, one section after that implementation repeated round 5's rule-pool bug. The audit's scoping is adopted verbatim: **L-REFINE-1 stands** — its 24 films all terminate `NORMAL_FORM`, the path that always re-derived — but the *protocol* was not closed over every terminal it accepts, so v1.0.0's "closed" claim was not yet true.

**9. Closed at the schema level, not the `if` level.** New law, `law:film.terminal-witness@1`: *every terminal class a replay accepts must have a complete declared witness schema; every witness field committed; every witness field independently re-derived.* `TRVM-SEMFILM-v1.1` commits `budget` and `remaining_work`; replay re-derives the budget terminal — integers, `remaining_work` equal to the live enabled count under the declared pool, and **tighter than execution films where the honest generator permits it**: `steps === budget` (the generator stops exactly there; `>=` would admit incoherent overruns) and `remaining_work > 0` (a budget claim on a quiescent state is an under-claim — tolerated in the local evidence object, refused in the portable one where the terminal class *is* the claim). The strictness delta is recorded in the grid, with the exec-film revisit explicitly deferred to a possible `film.evidence-chain@6`. The grid's schema ambiguity GPT flagged — an accepted outcome with no declared witness fields — is fixed in `semantic_film.terminal_fields`, and grid_check v2.4 now *checks schema completeness*: the declared terminal fields must carry both witnesses, the refusal vocabulary is 19 and presence-checked, and the kernel must carry the v1.1 domain tag.

**10. L-SEMTERM-1: twelve forgeries, one new positive.** The positive direction first: an honest 7-step **partial** semantic film replays on *both* allocators — portable checkpoints, the primitive long cross-implementation runs will need, and something v1.0.0 never demonstrated. Then the audit's battery, each case dying on its declared refusal: unsealed budget/work mutations now **change the commitment** (the exact property whose absence GPT measured) *and* refuse on re-derivation; resealed budget lie and steps-overrun (`sem-budget-mismatch`); negative, wrong, and pool-narrowed remaining work (`sem-terminal-work-mismatch` — the pool-narrowing case proves the declared rule pool participates in the work arithmetic); malformed budget (`sem-terminal-malformed`); a budget claim on a genuinely quiescent state and an NF→BUDGET flip (`sem-no-remaining-work`); a partial film relabeled `NORMAL_FORM` (`sem-false-normal-form`); and GPT's zero-frame witness, honestly resealed under v1.1, refused. The negative battery grew to **23 forgeries, 23 distinct diagnostics**, including grid-side tampers of the new refusal vocabulary and the terminal schema itself.

**The moral, stated once for the fourth implementation.** Twice now, a new replay implementation silently failed to inherit the correctness of its predecessors — the rule-pool check in round 6, the terminal contract in round 6.1. `law:film.terminal-witness@1` exists so that ic32, the fourth implementation, inherits the **obligation** instead of the assumption: the law is checkable against any replayer, and the grid's schema-completeness check makes "accepted but unproven" a structural impossibility rather than a code-review hope. v1.0.1 is the v1 claim, now true: kernel 30 status rows green, grid 27 entries / 153 citations / 8 artifacts, κ green, negative battery 23/23, cert and receipt ids deterministic.
